Domain Strategy & Digital Identity

The name is the anchor. Identity is everything attached to it.

A domain is the most durable piece of digital identity a business can own. Lucky Domains helps you secure the right one, sell one you no longer need, and make the identity built on it clear to people, search engines, and AI systems.

DomainEntity

yourname.com

Domains already work as identity anchors for people, companies, products, and online properties. Email, profiles, citations, and structured data all point back to the name.

Domain

The domain you want is rarely for sale until someone who knows how to ask comes along.

The name that is already taken, valued, negotiated, and transferred through escrow. Or a quiet sale of one you own.

  1. 01

    Set the range

    A fair-market range from comparable sales, traffic and keyword data, and strategic value, before anyone is contacted.

  2. 02

    Reach the owner

    The conversation opens without revealing who the buyer is, so the price stays grounded in value.

  3. 03

    Negotiate

    Against the range you agreed, not the seller's opening ask. You approve or decline the price at every step.

  4. 04

    Close through escrow

    Funds release only when the domain is verifiably in your control.

Selling a domain

You should not have to run a public auction to sell a good domain. Ken sets a fair-market range first, approaches qualified buyers confidentially, and handles the negotiation and transfer through third-party escrow.

Fee

Emerging research

Can domains and naming systems also become identity infrastructure for AI agents?

Software agents are starting to find services, pay for work, and act on someone's behalf. They will need some of what people and companies already get from a domain: a name others can read, and a way to check who stands behind it.

  • Human-readable identity

    A name a person can recognize, not only a key or an address.

  • Discovery

    A known place to look an agent up.

  • Service descriptions

    What an agent offers, in a form other software can read.

  • Ownership

    Who controls the name, and therefore the agent.

  • Wallets and payment destinations

    Where an agent is paid, tied to the same name.

  • Provenance

    Evidence that a result came from the agent it claims to come from.

  • Permissions

    What an agent is allowed to do, and for whom.

  • Reputation

    A record that travels with the name.

This is an open question, not a mature service. There is no settled standard, and Lucky Domains does not sell an agent-identity product. Ken explores it in public experiments, documented on KenAshe.ai.

Supporting experiments

  • LIVEZeroHumanA small service that sells two jobs to other AI agents. Its ENS name, zerohuman.eth, is the storefront, the payment address, and the sender of every result.As of November 20, 2026, no outside agent has bought from it.
  • SHIPPEDAI Agent EconomyTwo AI agents with their own wallets completed one purchase on Ethereum mainnet, with an ENS record as the storefront.Both agents were operated by Ken on one machine.
