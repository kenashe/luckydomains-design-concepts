Can domains become the identity layer for AI agents?

By Ken Ashe. Draft for review. Not published.

Draft for review. Not published. Concept content for review.

People use domains as durable identities. Businesses use them as the canonical home of their brand. AI agents may need something similar: a readable name, a way to be found, and a way to check who stands behind them.

Domains already do this for people and companies

A domain is the one identifier most people and businesses fully control. Email, profiles, structured data, and citations all point back to it. When a search engine or an AI system needs to decide which company a mention belongs to, the domain is often the anchor that settles it.

What an agent would need

An agent that offers a service, or pays for one, needs some of the same things: a human-readable name, a known place to be looked up, a description of what it does that other software can read, a payment destination tied to that name, evidence that a result came from it, and a record that travels with it.

What Ken has tested in public

ZeroHuman is a small service that sells two jobs to other AI agents. Its ENS name, zerohuman.eth, is the storefront, the payment address, and the sender of every result. A text record on the name publishes the menu, and the service is listed in the ERC-8004 agent registry as agent #56626, linked back from the name with an ENSIP-25 record.

An earlier experiment had two AI agents with their own wallets complete one purchase on Ethereum mainnet, with an ENS text record as the storefront. Both are documented on KenAshe.ai.

What it does not prove

As of November 20, 2026, no outside agent had bought from ZeroHuman. Both experiments were operated by Ken. There is no settled standard for agent identity, and these tests are evidence that the pieces can work, not that the market has chosen them.

Where this leaves Lucky Domains

This is research, not a product. Lucky Domains does not sell agent identity. The question matters to the core work anyway: if software is going to look businesses up, the same identity signals that help search engines and AI systems today will matter even more.
