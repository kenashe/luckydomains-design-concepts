Version 3: built from the original site

Three new directions that start from the live Lucky Domains design, not from a new one. Each loads the production stylesheet itself and adds a layer on top. All three tell the same story, offer the same services, and have the same sixteen pages, so the comparison is about design and interaction.

These are proposed design directions only. They do not modify LuckyDomains.io, KenAshe.ai, production repositories, DNS, forms, or integrations.

What changed from Phase 2

Phase 2 drifted away from the original look and built only six pages per concept. Version 3 keeps the original design (navy hero, white and soft-grey sections, icon cards, green checks, numbered steps, the green call-to-action band, and the navy footer), builds a fuller site, and adds motion that explains the business instead of showcasing experiments.

Shared positioning

Lucky Domains helps businesses build a digital presence that people, search engines, and AI systems can find, understand, and trust.

The original site described the work as Domain, Site, Rankings. Version 3 keeps that order and adds the two layers that now decide how a business is understood: Entity and AI.

The Digital Presence Stack

  1. Domain Own the name and anchor the identity.
  2. Website Build the destination the business controls.
  3. Search Make the site technically sound, relevant, and discoverable.
  4. Entity Help platforms understand who you are and how your website, profiles, founders, reviews, and references relate.
  5. AI Make the brand easier for AI-powered discovery to understand, verify, cite, and surface.

Five services, sixteen pages

  • Website Design & Development
  • SEO & Search Visibility
  • AI Visibility & Brand Authority
  • Entity SEO & Digital Identity
  • Domain Acquisition & Brokerage

Pages in every concept: Home, Services, Website Design, SEO, AI Visibility, Entity SEO, Domains, How we buy domains, Work, Case study, About, Founder, Insights, Research note, News & media, Contact.

Concept A

Original, Evolved

The production site, kept recognizable and modernized. Same navy hero, white and soft-grey bands, icon cards, green checks, numbered steps, green call-to-action band, and navy footer, with larger type, more room, and the three signature interactions.

  • Closest to the current site
  • Original stylesheet underneath
  • Navy hero, white sections
  • Layer bar that follows you
  • Most conventional to ship
Motion and interaction
A recognition map in the hero sends pulses from the brand to people, search engines, and AI systems, and leans toward the cursor. The Digital Presence Stack lights layer by layer as it scrolls past. A sticky layer bar highlights Domain, Website, Search, Entity, and AI as you read each homepage section. Plus the Where are you stuck? diagnostic.
Best suited for
Shipping with the least risk. It feels like the company you already built, redesigned for 2027.
Tradeoff
The least new. It improves the current site rather than giving it a new signature.

Concept B

Original, Editorial

The same original components, set for reading. A large left-aligned headline, the Lucky Domains story told as five layers in giant type, services as numbered editorial rows, an annotated case study, and insights given real space.

  • Same palette and fonts
  • Left-aligned, larger type
  • Contents rail on inner pages
  • Annotated case study
  • Best home for writing
Motion and interaction
Reading progress on every page, and a section-aware contents rail on every inner page. The five layer words come into full weight as they cross the middle of the screen. A before-and-after identity example alternates on its own and stops once you choose. Case-study notes highlight on hover, focus, or tap.
Best suited for
A business that will publish case studies and guides, and wants the site to read as expertise.
Tradeoff
Needs writing to stay strong. Long service pages ask more of the reader than A.

Concept C

Original, Interactive

The original design with the most interaction. A living presence map in the navy hero shows how a domain, a website, content, search, structured data, profiles, reviews, founders, and citations come together into one identity, and the rest of the site lets visitors explore it.

  • Same palette and fonts
  • Presence map in the hero
  • Layers light the map as you scroll
  • Quick presence self-check
  • Example checks on every service page
Motion and interaction
The hero map settles from fragmented to recognized, then tours one signal at a time; pick a problem and it lights the signals involved. A second map lights each layer as you read it. Example-check lists step through on their own and pause on interaction. A six-question self-check updates as you tick boxes.
Best suited for
Showing, not telling, how entity work and AI visibility connect. The most memorable demonstration of the service.
Tradeoff
The most to build and maintain, and the busiest homepage. The map has to stay an explanation, not a dashboard.
